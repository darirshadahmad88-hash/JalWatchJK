    Early warning for agricultural water stress

    The water table drops months
    before the mandi price does.

    Jal Watch reads CGWB's telemetric groundwater sensors across saffron and apple-growing districts of Jammu & Kashmir, and lines them up against years of Agmarknet mandi records — turning a slow, quiet decline underground into a warning that officials and growers can act on before harvest.

    Overview

    Two feeds, one signal, months of lead time

    ▾
    01

    Groundwater telemetry

    Digital Water Level Recorders (DWLR?) under CGWB's National Hydrology Project transmit depth-to-water readings every six hours, surfaced publicly through India-WRIS. We track the districts behind J&K's saffron and apple economy.

    02

    Mandi price history

    Agmarknet? publishes daily arrivals and modal prices across 19 J&K APMC? markets. Years of this record let us see how past droughts and extraction stress actually showed up at the market — not just in theory.

    03

    A stress score, not a prophecy

    We compute a seasonal z-score? for water level per district, adjusted for rainfall, and show its historical relationship to arrivals. It's a decision-support signal for officials and growers — deliberately not framed as a price forecast.

    Methodology

    How the stress index is built

    ▾
    1. 1

      Match the signal to the crop, not just the sensor

      Saffron corms sit close to the aquifer, so Pampore's index still runs on DWLR depth-to-water. Apple orchards in Kashmir are largely rain-fed — growers and agronomists report they respond to root-zone soil moisture and winter snowpack, not the water table directly. So Shopian, Sopore, and Pulwama are scored on a soil-moisture series (derived from rainfall, with a damped lag matching how moisture actually persists in soil) instead of raw groundwater depth. Each is baselined against its own historical same-season values, not a flat annual average.

    2. 2

      Adjust for rainfall

      A dry monsoon and over-extraction look identical in raw water-level data. Overlaying IMD rainfall lets the index distinguish "drought year" from "structural decline" — the second is the one worth escalating.

    3. 3

      Score, don't predict

      The output is a z-score bucketed into normal / watch / stress / severe, alongside the closest historical analogue season and what happened to arrivals and prices then. No price forecast is generated — the historical parallel is left for a human to judge.

    4. 4

      Alert two audiences differently

      Farmers get a short, local-language irrigation-planning nudge. District officials and APMC administrators get the full dashboard for procurement and drought-relief planning.

    5. 5

      The top-of-page signal is a formula, not a model

      The "next-season signal" shown at the top of this page is a documented heuristic — it moves in proportion to how far current water and rainfall readings sit from their seasonal average, using constants we chose and disclosed rather than fitted from data. It's there to make the water-price relationship legible at a glance, not to claim predictive accuracy.

    Data sources

    Real, public, structured

    ▾

    CGWB / India-WRIS

    Groundwater level readings from telemetric DWLR stations under the National Hydrology Project. Surfaced via the India-WRIS public portal.

    indiawris.gov.in →

    Agmarknet / data.gov.in

    Daily mandi arrivals and price data across J&K's 19 APMC markets, available through a documented public API.

    data.gov.in →

    IMD rainfall (optional layer)

    District-level rainfall used to separate drought-driven decline from over-extraction in the stress index.

    mausam.imd.gov.in →

    NASA POWER (root-zone soil wetness?)

    Live current reading for apple districts via the free, keyless GWETROOT parameter (MERRA-2 reanalysis) — the historical trend line is still derived from demo rainfall; see README for backfilling it with real POWER data.

    power.larc.nasa.gov →

    Google Flood Hub / CWC (Jhelum flood status)

    The top-right Flood Watch widget reads the Jhelum gauge at Ram Munshi Bagh — severity, trend, and threshold levels sourced from CWC via Google's Flood Forecasting API. Needs a waitlisted API key; shows a clearly-labelled demo reading until one is set. See README.

    sites.research.google/floods →
